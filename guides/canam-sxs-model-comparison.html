Buying Guide

Maverick R vs. Maverick X3 vs. Defender vs. Commander

Four very different machines wear the Can-Am badge. Which one actually fits you comes down to one question: are you racing terrain, playing on trail, or putting the machine to work?

Updated: September 2026 Read time: 12 min Best for: First-time buyers, cross-shoppers

Quick answer: Choose the Maverick R for the fastest, most race-derived open-terrain performance money can buy. Choose the Maverick X3 for sport-focused trail and dune riding with serious suspension travel at a lower cost of entry than the R. Choose the Defender if hauling, towing, and property or farm work come first. Choose the Commander if you want a single machine that splits time between weekend trail rides and light utility duty.

Four Lineups Built for Four Different Jobs

It's easy to assume every Can-Am SxS is competing for the same buyer, but the four core lineups were engineered around genuinely different priorities — not just different price points. The Maverick R and Maverick X3 are built around suspension travel, horsepower, and high-speed stability. The Defender and Commander are built around payload, towing capacity, and day-to-day usability. Cross-shopping across those two groups usually means comparing machines that were never meant to solve the same problem.

Before comparing spec sheets, it helps to sort by intended use first:

  • Sport / race terrain — Maverick R, for open desert, high-speed whoops, and competition-style riding.
  • Sport / trail and dune — Maverick X3, for spirited trail riding, dunes, and weekend performance driving.
  • Utility / work — Defender, for towing, hauling, and property or ranch tasks.
  • Utility / recreation crossover — Commander, for riders who want one machine that does a bit of both.
Buyer tip

Write down your three most common use cases before you shop — trail miles per weekend, whether you'll tow or haul, and whether top speed or payload matters more. That short list narrows these four options faster than any spec sheet will.

Maverick R: Race-Bred Performance

1Maverick R

Flagship Sport
Best ForOpen desert, high-speed terrain
EngineTurbocharged Rotax triple
Standout TraitRace-derived suspension

The Maverick R sits at the top of the Can-Am lineup as its most extreme performance machine, built with technology drawn directly from Can-Am's factory racing program. It's the widest-stance, longest-travel machine in the lineup, engineered for riders who spend serious time at speed across rough, open terrain rather than tight technical trail.

Why Riders Choose It

  • Industry-leading ground clearance and suspension travel built for high-speed rough terrain
  • A rapid-shifting transmission designed to hold power delivery steady at speed
  • Multiple drive modes, including a dedicated performance mode for aggressive throttle response
  • The clear choice for riders who want the most race-capable machine Can-Am builds, full stop

Who Should Look Elsewhere

  • Riders who mostly stick to tight, technical, tree-lined trail — the R's width and speed focus go underused there
  • Buyers who need towing or cargo capacity, since this lineup isn't built around utility features
  • First-time SxS owners looking for a lower-cost entry point into the sport

Maverick X3: Sport Riding for Trail and Dune

2Maverick X3

Sport / Trail
Best ForTrail, dune, spirited weekend riding
EngineTurbocharged Rotax triple
Standout TraitPower-to-weight ratio

The Maverick X3 has long been Can-Am's flagship sport machine, and it remains the more accessible performance option now that the Maverick R occupies the top tier. It's lighter and narrower than the R, which makes it noticeably more nimble in tighter trail sections while still delivering serious horsepower and dune-ready suspension travel.

Why Riders Choose It

  • A lighter, narrower chassis than the Maverick R, better suited to trail systems with tighter turns
  • A strong power-to-weight ratio that holds its own on dunes, whoops, and fast desert trail
  • A wide range of trims and suspension packages, so buyers can dial in performance versus budget
  • A lower cost of entry into serious sport performance than the range-topping R

Who Should Look Elsewhere

  • Riders chasing the absolute top of the performance ceiling — that's what the Maverick R is built for
  • Buyers who need real cargo bed capacity or towing rating for work tasks
  • Anyone prioritizing passenger comfort and cabin space over outright performance

Defender: The Utility Workhorse

3Defender

Utility
Best ForTowing, hauling, property work
EngineRotax naturally aspirated / turbo options
Standout TraitCargo bed & towing capacity

The Defender is built first as a work machine and second as a recreational one — and that priority shows in everything from its bed capacity to its low-end torque delivery. It's the widest and most stable machine in the lineup at low speed, which also happens to make it a genuinely good option for new riders who spend more time working a property than chasing speed.

Why Riders Choose It

  • A true cargo bed and towing rating built around real farm, ranch, and property work
  • Stable, planted handling at low speed thanks to its wide stance
  • Strong low-end torque suited to plowing, dragging equipment, and climbing loaded
  • A wide range of cab configurations, from open-air to fully enclosed for year-round use

Who Should Look Elsewhere

  • Riders whose primary goal is speed or technical trail performance
  • Buyers who want dune or whoop capability — that's not what this chassis is tuned for
  • Anyone who won't regularly use the bed, towing capacity, or work-focused accessories

Commander: The Trail-and-Task Crossover

4Commander

Utility / Recreation
Best ForMixed trail riding & light utility
EngineRotax naturally aspirated / turbo options
Standout TraitBalance of ride comfort & capability

The Commander sits deliberately between the Defender's work focus and the Maverick line's sport focus. It carries a smaller bed and lighter towing rating than the Defender, but trades that for a more trail-oriented ride feel and a more engaging drive — a genuine middle-ground pick for riders who don't want to choose between hauling gear and enjoying the ride there.

Why Riders Choose It

  • A more spirited, trail-oriented ride than the Defender without giving up a functional cargo bed
  • A comfortable middle ground for riders who split time between weekend trail rides and light hauling
  • Available in performance-leaning trims for riders who want a sportier feel without stepping into the Maverick line
  • A strong choice for buyers who genuinely can't decide between "work rig" and "trail rig"

Who Should Look Elsewhere

  • Buyers with heavy, regular towing or hauling needs — the Defender is built to carry more
  • Riders chasing dedicated sport or race performance — the Maverick line exists for that
  • Anyone who only needs one specific use case rather than a balance of two

Side-by-Side: How the Four Compare

Once you know your primary use case, these are the categories that actually separate the four lineups in real-world ownership:

Primary Strength by Model

  • Maverick R — outright speed and suspension travel across rough, open terrain
  • Maverick X3 — a lighter, more agile take on sport performance for trail and dune
  • Defender — maximum cargo, towing, and low-speed stability for real work
  • Commander — the most balanced option between trail enjoyment and light utility

Where Each Model Falls Short

  • Maverick R — least practical for hauling, towing, or tight technical trail
  • Maverick X3 — limited cargo and passenger-comfort features compared to utility models
  • Defender — the least sport-oriented ride feel and lowest top-end performance ceiling
  • Commander — carries less cargo and tows less than the Defender, despite the similar body style
Buyer tip

Test ride more than one trim within a lineup before deciding against an entire model line. A base Defender and a performance-package Commander can feel closer to each other on trail than two trims within the same nameplate.

How to Choose Based on How You'll Actually Ride

Spec sheets matter less than an honest answer to how the machine will actually spend its weekends. Work through these questions in order:

  1. Will you regularly tow or haul a real load? If yes, start with the Defender before considering anything else.
  2. Is top speed and suspension travel across open terrain the priority? If yes, the Maverick R is built specifically for that use case.
  3. Do you want strong sport performance without the Maverick R's price and width? The Maverick X3 covers that ground.
  4. Do you need one machine that reasonably handles both trail fun and occasional hauling? The Commander is built as that middle ground.
  5. Are you brand new to SxS riding? The Defender or a lower-trim Commander offer the most forgiving learning curve of the four.

Mistakes Buyers Make Cross-Shopping These Models

Buying a Maverick for work capacity it was never built to have

Both Maverick lineups prioritize performance over cargo and towing. If hauling matters, the Defender or Commander will serve you far better long-term.

Buying a Defender expecting Maverick-level trail performance

The Defender's strength is stability and utility, not speed. Riders chasing a sportier feel are usually happier in a Commander or Maverick X3.

Assuming the Maverick R is simply a "faster X3"

The R is a different platform with a different focus — wider stance, longer travel, and race-derived components. It's not a straightforward upgrade so much as a different tool for different terrain.

Overlooking the Commander because it looks like "just a smaller Defender"

The Commander's ride tuning and available performance trims set it apart from the Defender's utility-first character — it deserves its own test ride, not a pass based on appearance alone.

Choosing based on horsepower numbers alone

Weight, width, suspension travel, and gearing affect how power actually translates to the trail. A side-by-side test ride tells you more than a spec sheet comparison ever will.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the main difference between the Maverick R and Maverick X3?

The Maverick R is Can-Am's flagship performance platform, built with a wider stance and longer suspension travel for high-speed open terrain. The Maverick X3 is lighter and narrower, making it more agile on tighter trail while still delivering strong sport performance at a lower cost of entry.

Is the Defender or Commander better for towing?

The Defender is the stronger choice for towing and hauling, with a larger cargo bed and higher towing rating built specifically around work tasks. The Commander offers a smaller bed and lighter towing capacity in exchange for a more trail-oriented ride feel.

Which Can-Am model is best for a first-time buyer?

The Defender or a base-trim Commander tend to suit first-time buyers best, thanks to their stable low-speed handling and forgiving learning curve. The Maverick line is better suited to riders who already have some off-road driving experience.

Can the Maverick X3 or Maverick R be used for trail riding, not just racing?

Yes, both are commonly used for recreational trail riding, not only competition. The Maverick X3's narrower stance makes it better suited to tighter trail systems, while the wider Maverick R is most at home on open, higher-speed terrain.

Is the Commander just a smaller version of the Defender?

Not exactly — while they share a similar body style, the Commander is tuned for a more spirited, trail-oriented ride and offered in performance-leaning trims, while the Defender is tuned first around cargo capacity and work capability.
