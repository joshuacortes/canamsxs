Top Off-Road Trails for Can-Am SxS Riders

A roundup of the most popular trail systems for Can-Am SxS riders, ranked by terrain, difficulty, and what you'll want in the truck bed before you tow up — from beginner-friendly forest routes to expert-only rock gardens.

Updated: September 2026 Read time: 11 min Best for: Maverick X3, Maverick R, Defender, Commander

Quick answer: The Hatfield-McCoy Trail System in West Virginia, the Paiute ATV Trail in Utah, and Brimstone Recreation in Tennessee are the three highest-rated destinations for Can-Am SxS riders in 2026, thanks to their trail mileage, terrain variety, and machine-width access. Below, we break down seven top-tier trail systems by difficulty, what to expect, and what to pack.

What Makes a Trail Great for a Can-Am SxS?

A great Can-Am SxS trail comes down to four things: enough width for your machine, terrain that matches your skill level, reliable access to fuel and repairs, and a land manager that actually maintains the routes. A stunning trail system means nothing if your Maverick X3's 68-inch stance gets shut out at the trailhead, so width restrictions are the first thing to check — not the scenery.

Before you commit a weekend to a destination, run it against this checklist:

  • Width clearance — confirm the posted limit fits your model; Defenders and Commander XTs often exceed 50" ATV-only trails.
  • Terrain variety — the best systems mix graded gravel, technical rock, mud, and elevation so a multi-day trip doesn't get repetitive.
  • Fuel and parts access — trailhead towns with gas stations, tire shops, and dealer support matter more than they sound.
  • Permit and sticker requirements — many state-run systems require a trail permit or OHV registration sticker purchased in advance.
  • Seasonal conditions — desert systems ride best outside summer heat; mountain systems often close for mud season or snow.

The 7 Best Trail Systems for Can-Am SxS Riders

These seven destinations consistently rank at the top for SxS riders because they combine real trail mileage with terrain that rewards a Can-Am's suspension travel and width. Each entry includes a difficulty rating, standout features, and who the trail is really built for.

1Hatfield-McCoy Trail System — West Virginia

Easy to Extreme
Trail Mileage1,000+ miles
TerrainMountain, hardwood forest, rock
Best SeasonSpring–Fall

Hatfield-McCoy is the largest interconnected off-road trail system in the country, and it's the default answer when riders ask where to take a Can-Am for a full weekend rather than an afternoon. The system is split into multiple trail areas — Rockhouse, Pocahontas, Bearwallow, and others — so you can string together an entire trip without loading the trailer twice.

What Stands Out

  • Nine separate trail areas connected by dedicated OHV roads between towns
  • Trailhead towns with fuel, lodging, and powersports repair shops built around the trail economy
  • Terrain ranges from smooth gravel fire roads to genuinely difficult rock ledges

Good to Know

  • Requires a Hatfield-McCoy trail permit, purchased online or at local vendors
  • Wet weather turns clay sections greasy fast — check recent rainfall before you go

2Paiute ATV Trail — Utah

Easy to Moderate
Trail Mileage275-mile main loop
TerrainAlpine forest, high desert
Best SeasonSummer

The Paiute is built around a 275-mile main loop through central Utah's mountains, with more than 900 miles of connecting side trails and forest routes feeding into it. It's the rare system that's genuinely welcoming to newer riders while still offering enough side-trail mileage to keep experienced Can-Am owners exploring for a week straight.

What Stands Out

  • Elevation changes from high desert sagebrush to alpine forest above 9,000 feet
  • Five trailhead towns — Marysvale, Richfield, Beaver, Fillmore, and Salina — spaced along the route
  • Mostly wide, well-graded trail bed suited to full-size Commanders and Defenders

Good to Know

  • Summer afternoons bring thunderstorms at elevation — plan morning riding blocks
  • Some side trails narrow considerably; check width postings before detouring

3Brimstone Recreation — Tennessee

Moderate to Difficult
Trail Mileage300+ miles
TerrainAppalachian ridgeline, creek crossings
Best SeasonSpring–Fall

Brimstone sits on former coal-mining land in the Cumberland Mountains, and the old haul roads make for wide, sweeping trails that climb ridgelines with long-range views. It's a favorite for Maverick X3 owners who want technical sections without needing a spotter every mile.

What Stands Out

  • On-site campground and cabins within walking distance of trailheads
  • A mix of scenic ridge roads and genuinely challenging rock and creek sections
  • Well-marked difficulty ratings posted at every trail junction

Good to Know

  • Day passes and annual permits are both available; day passes sell out on holiday weekends
  • Cell service is spotty past the first few miles — download offline maps first

4Rausch Creek Off-Road Park — Pennsylvania

Easy to Extreme
Trail Mileage50+ miles
TerrainReclaimed strip-mine rock fields
Best SeasonYear-round (weather permitting)

Rausch Creek is smaller in total mileage than the mega-systems above, but it packs an outsized reputation for rock crawling. Built on reclaimed strip-mine land, the park offers a genuine progression — from graded beginner loops to black-diamond and extreme-rated rock gardens that push a fully built Maverick X3 X rs Turbo RR to its limits.

What Stands Out

  • Rated trail progression, similar to a ski resort's green-to-black system
  • Dedicated rock-crawling sections with named obstacles and spotter areas
  • Membership and day-pass options with a large base camp and vendor area

Good to Know

  • Extreme trails can require a winch, rock sliders, and a spotter — this isn't a stock-machine playground on the black routes
  • Book campsites ahead for event weekends; the park hosts organized rides throughout the season

5Little Sahara Recreation Area — Utah

Moderate
Trail Mileage60,000+ acres of dunes
TerrainOpen sand dunes
Best SeasonFall–Spring

Little Sahara is the go-to destination if you want to run wide open instead of picking a line through rocks. Its dune fields cover tens of thousands of acres split into distinct riding zones, giving SxS drivers room to actually use a Maverick R's power without a trail width limiting the line.

What Stands Out

  • Massive open terrain with no fixed trail lines — ride your own route
  • Sand Mountain, a 700-foot dune, is the park's signature challenge
  • Designated family-friendly zones separate from the high-speed dune areas

Good to Know

  • Paddle or sand tires make a real difference here over stock trail tires
  • Summer sand surface temperatures climb high enough to affect tire pressure — ride spring, fall, or winter

6Hidden Falls Adventure Park — Texas

Moderate to Extreme
Trail Mileage~240 miles
TerrainHill Country limestone, boulder fields
Best SeasonFall–Spring

Hidden Falls covers roughly 3,000 acres of Texas Hill Country with nearly 240 miles of marked trail, and almost all of it is open to full-size side-by-sides. The Wildcat Mountain area is the park's headline attraction — a boulder-strewn climb that ranks among the toughest full-size trails in the country.

What Stands Out

  • Wildcat Stairway — a top-rated technical rock climb open to full-size SxS machines
  • On-site lodging, restaurant, and a large event calendar throughout the year
  • Hill Country scenery that's a genuine change of pace from typical desert or mountain trail systems

Good to Know

  • Texas summer heat is brutal on riders and machines alike — plan cooler-season trips
  • Reservations recommended on weekends; the park's popularity means it fills up fast

7Drummond Island — Michigan

Easy to Moderate
Trail MileageClosed-loop island network
TerrainNorthern forest, wetlands, ridges
Best SeasonSummer–Fall

Michigan's largest island in Lake Huron holds the state's most extensive closed-loop off-road trail network, and it's an underrated pick for riders who want a genuinely scenic, low-stress week on a Commander or Defender rather than a technical proving ground.

What Stands Out

  • A true closed-loop network — ride out and back to the same lodging without repeating trails
  • Wildflower glades, dense forest, and rugged ridgelines in one compact area
  • Ferry access keeps traffic lower than mainland trail systems of similar size

Good to Know

  • Plan around the ferry schedule — it's the only way on and off the island
  • Fall color season is stunning but also the busiest — book lodging early

How Trail Difficulty Ratings Work

Most U.S. trail systems rate difficulty on a scale borrowed from ski resorts — green, blue, black, and double-black — though the exact labels vary by land manager. Here's how to read them before you commit your Can-Am to a route:

  1. Easy (Green): Wide, graded surfaces — gravel, packed dirt, gentle grades. Suitable for any stock Can-Am SxS and new riders.
  2. Moderate (Blue): Narrower trail bed, some loose rock and moderate grades. A stock machine handles this fine with an attentive driver.
  3. Difficult (Black): Steep grades, embedded rock, mud holes, and off-camber sections. Aftermarket tires, skid plates, and rock sliders are worth having.
  4. Extreme (Double Black): Rock crawling, deep ruts, and obstacles that may require a winch or a spotter. Built or heavily upgraded machines only.

A rating is only as good as the person who assigned it, and conditions shift trail-to-trail after rain or seasonal wear. Treat posted ratings as a starting point, not a guarantee, and always ask at the trailhead office about current conditions before you commit to a black or double-black route.

What to Bring on a Can-Am SxS Trail Ride

Pack by category, not by memory — it's easy to forget recovery gear when you're focused on fuel and food. Here's the checklist worth running through before every trip, whether it's a Saturday loop or a week at Hatfield-McCoy.

Safety & Personal Gear

  • DOT-rated helmet and eye protection for every rider
  • Gloves, over-the-ankle boots, and long sleeves/pants even in summer
  • First-aid kit sized for the group, not just the driver
  • Layered clothing — mountain and desert temperature swings are significant morning to afternoon

Recovery & Repair

  • Tow strap or recovery rope rated for your machine's weight
  • Tire repair kit and a portable 12V air compressor
  • Basic tool kit, spare fuses, and duct tape
  • Winch, if you're planning anything rated Difficult or Extreme

Navigation & Communication

  • GPS unit or offline trail maps downloaded before you lose signal
  • Satellite communicator or two-way radios for group rides in dead zones
  • Physical trail map as backup — batteries die, paper doesn't

Fuel & Fluids

  • Extra fuel in an approved container, sized to the day's mileage plus a margin
  • Drinking water — more than feels necessary, especially at elevation or in dune country
  • Spare oil or coolant for longer, multi-day trips

Trail Etiquette & Safety Tips

  1. Yield uphill traffic the right of way. It's harder for a climbing machine to stop and restart than a descending one.
  2. Stay on marked trails. Cutting switchbacks accelerates erosion and is how trail systems lose access permits.
  3. Ride within your headlights and sightlines. Blind curves on multi-use trails mean oncoming traffic with no warning.
  4. Pack out everything you pack in. Trail systems funded by permit fees stay open because riders keep them clean.
  5. Tell someone your route and return time before you head out, especially on remote systems with poor cell coverage.
  6. Check width and vehicle-class postings at every junction — restrictions can change trail-to-trail within the same system.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best off-road trail system for a Can-Am SxS?

Hatfield-McCoy in West Virginia is the best all-around pick, with over 1,000 miles of connected trail spanning easy gravel roads to expert rock climbs, plus trailhead towns built around fuel, lodging, and repairs.

Can a Can-Am Maverick X3 fit on ATV-only trails?

It comes down to the posted width limit, not the brand. Trails restricted to 50 inches or less will exclude a Maverick X3 or Commander XT, since both run wider than that — always check before you tow up.

What should I bring on a Can-Am SxS trail ride?

A helmet and eye protection, a first-aid kit, recovery gear like a tow strap, a tire repair kit with an air compressor, extra fuel, offline trail maps, and layered clothing. Add a satellite communicator for remote, no-signal routes.

What do UTV trail difficulty ratings mean?

Difficulty is typically rated Easy, Moderate, Difficult, or Extreme — similar to ski slope colors. Easy trails are wide and graded; Extreme trails involve rock crawling and obstacles that may require a winch or a spotter.

Is Rausch Creek Off-Road Park good for beginners?

Yes — Rausch Creek offers graded beginner loops alongside its famous rock-crawling sections, so new riders can progress toward the park's black-diamond and extreme terrain at their own pace.
