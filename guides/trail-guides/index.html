Trail Guides

Where to ride, what to expect, and how to plan your next trip.

Guides in this category: 3 Best for: Trip planning, new riders, group rides

Every Can-Am SxS trail rides differently depending on surface, elevation, and how the trail system is rated — which makes "just go find a trail" some of the worst advice a new rider can follow. These guides cover the trail systems worth planning a trip around, how to build confidence if you're new to the sport, and the safety and etiquette expectations that keep shared multi-use trails open for everyone riding them.

How to Use These Guides to Plan a Real Trip

Trail difficulty ratings, terrain, and best-season windows vary significantly by region, so the right guide to start with depends on where you're at as a rider, not just where you're headed.

If You're Planning Your Next Destination

  • Check the difficulty rating against your comfort level, not just the scenery photos
  • Confirm the best season for the specific trail system — conditions shift more than most riders expect between spring and fall
  • Look for nearby bail-out or shorter loop options in case the group wants to split up mid-ride

If You're Still Building Confidence

  • Start with Easy-rated mileage before mixing in anything Moderate or harder
  • Ride a full easy loop start to finish before judging whether you're ready to progress
  • Bring someone more experienced along for your first few outings on unfamiliar terrain

If You're Riding With a Group or on Shared Trails

  • Review right-of-way rules before you're on the trail, not mid-ride
  • Pack the same core safety gear regardless of trail difficulty rating
  • Confirm a group communication plan for pace, stops, and what to do if someone falls behind

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the best way to find Can-Am SxS trails near me?

Start with a nationally recognized trail roundup to see well-rated systems by region, then confirm current conditions and permit requirements directly with the trail system's official land-manager office before you go.

Are Can-Am trail difficulty ratings the same everywhere?

Ratings follow similar general categories — Easy, Moderate, Difficult, Extreme — but the terrain behind each rating can vary noticeably between trail systems and regions, so it's worth reading a trail-specific guide rather than relying on the rating label alone.

What should I pack for a group SxS trail ride?

At minimum: a DOT-rated helmet, eye protection, gloves, a paper or offline trail map, water, and a portable phone battery. Our trail etiquette and safety guide covers the full list along with group-riding expectations.
