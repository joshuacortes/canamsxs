New vs. Used Can-Am SxS: Which Should You Buy?

A used Defender or Maverick can save you real money up front — but a lost warranty, a higher interest rate, and an unknown maintenance history can quietly erase that savings. Here's how to actually run the math before you decide.

Updated: September 2026 Read time: 11 min Best for: First-time buyers, trade-up shoppers

Quick answer: Buy new if you want full factory warranty coverage, promotional financing rates, and a machine with zero unknown history — the price premium buys predictability. Buy used if you're comfortable inspecting a machine carefully (or paying for a pre-purchase inspection) and want to avoid the steepest depreciation, which typically hits a Can-Am SxS hardest in its first one to two years on the road.

The Real Question Isn't Price — It's Total Cost

Sticker price is the number everyone compares, but it's rarely the number that decides whether new or used was the smarter buy. The real comparison is total cost of ownership: purchase price, plus financing cost, minus whatever warranty coverage protects you from a major repair bill, minus resale value when you eventually sell or trade it in. A used SxS with a lapsed warranty and a rough maintenance history can end up costing more than a new one once you factor in what breaks in year two.

Four variables drive that total cost more than any others:

  • Purchase price — the number on the tag, before tax, freight, and setup fees.
  • Financing terms — the interest rate and loan length attached to that price.
  • Warranty coverage — what's protected, for how long, and whether it transfers to a second owner.
  • Depreciation curve — how much value the machine has already lost, and how much it will lose next.
Buyer tip

Before comparing listings, decide how many hours or miles a year you'll realistically ride. Low annual use changes this math significantly — a used machine with light hours can be a much safer bet than one with the same age but heavy trail time.

How Fast Does a Can-Am SxS Actually Depreciate?

Powersports vehicles depreciate faster than cars in the first year, then the curve flattens out considerably. A new Can-Am typically loses the largest chunk of its value the moment it leaves the dealership and over its first season of use, since that's when it stops being "new" in the eyes of the next buyer. After that initial drop, well-maintained units tend to hold value much more steadily, especially popular trims like the Defender HD10 and Maverick X3 that stay in high demand on the used market.

What Speeds Up or Slows Down Depreciation

  • Hours/mileage: a lightly used two-year-old unit holds value far better than a heavily ridden one-year-old unit.
  • Trim popularity: in-demand trims (X3, HD10) tend to depreciate slower than base models because resale demand stays high.
  • Modifications: aftermarket parts rarely add resale value dollar-for-dollar, and non-reversible mods can actually shrink your buyer pool.
  • Documented maintenance: a full service record slows the resale value drop more than almost any other factor, because it reduces buyer risk.
Buyer tip

The sweet spot for used-market value is usually a two- to four-year-old unit — old enough that the first-year depreciation hit is already absorbed by the previous owner, young enough that it likely still has some factory warranty life or an extended plan attached.

Warranty Coverage: New vs. Used

This is where the new-vs-used decision gets the most expensive to get wrong. A drivetrain or electrical failure on an out-of-warranty SxS can run into thousands of dollars, and it's the single biggest hidden cost buyers underestimate when chasing a used-market discount.

New Can-Am SxS Warranty

  • Full factory limited warranty coverage from day one, backed directly by the manufacturer
  • Eligible for extended service contracts at the time of purchase, often at a lower rate than adding one later
  • No unknowns — every mile and every hour of use happens on your watch

Used Can-Am SxS Warranty

  • May still carry the remainder of the original factory warranty if it's within the coverage window — always ask for the VIN warranty lookup, don't take a seller's word for it
  • Extended warranties are sometimes transferable to a second owner, but not always — confirm this in writing before you buy
  • Once any factory or extended coverage lapses, every repair comes out of pocket, and used units are more likely to be closer to that line
Buyer tip

Never assume warranty coverage — verify it. Can-Am dealers can run a VIN check that shows exactly what's covered and through what date, and this takes only a few minutes before you commit to a used purchase.

Financing a New vs. Used Can-Am

Financing terms can shift the total cost of a purchase as much as the sticker price itself, and new and used units are rarely financed on equal footing.

Financing a New Unit

  • Manufacturer promotional rates are typically only available on new inventory, and can meaningfully lower total interest paid over the loan
  • Longer loan terms are usually available, which can lower the monthly payment if that matters more to your budget than total interest
  • Dealer financing on new units often bundles more easily with an extended warranty or gap coverage

Financing a Used Unit

  • Interest rates on used powersports loans typically run higher than new-unit promotional rates
  • Loan terms are often shorter, which raises the monthly payment even when the purchase price is lower
  • Private-party purchases may require a personal loan rather than dealer financing, which can mean a higher rate still
Buyer tip

Run the actual numbers before deciding on price alone: a used unit at $4,000 less but a meaningfully higher interest rate can cost about the same — or more — over the life of the loan than financing new at a promotional rate. Ask for the total-cost-of-loan number, not just the payment, before you compare offers.

When Each Option Actually Makes Sense

There's no universally correct answer here — the right call depends on how you use the machine, how much risk you're comfortable carrying, and how long you plan to keep it.

1Buy New If...

Lower Risk
  • You want the exact trim, color, and options package rather than whatever's available used
  • You plan to ride hard and often, and want full warranty coverage while you're putting real hours on the machine
  • You qualify for a manufacturer promotional financing rate that closes most of the price gap with used
  • You want zero unknowns on maintenance history, accident history, or prior modifications
  • You plan to keep it long enough that the first-year depreciation hit matters less over your total ownership period

2Buy Used If...

Higher Upside
  • You want to avoid paying for the steepest part of the depreciation curve yourself
  • You're comfortable inspecting a used machine thoroughly, or paying a dealer for a pre-purchase inspection
  • You find a unit with remaining factory or transferable extended warranty coverage
  • You have a clear maintenance and service history to review, not just a seller's description
  • You're upgrading trims or features and don't need the newest model year to be satisfied

Pre-Purchase Inspection Checklist

If you're leaning used, a careful inspection is what actually protects your savings. Go through this list in person before you agree to a price — not after:

  1. Pull the VIN warranty status. Confirm exactly what factory or extended coverage remains, and get it in writing.
  2. Ask for full service records. Oil changes, belt inspections, and any warranty repairs should all be documented — gaps are a red flag.
  3. Check belt and CVT condition. This is one of the most common wear points on a Can-Am SxS and one of the more expensive repairs to defer.
  4. Inspect the undercarriage. Look for cracked skid plates, bent A-arms, or signs of hard impacts that a seller may not mention unprompted.
  5. Test every electrical system. Lights, gauges, power steering, and any aftermarket accessories should all function correctly during your test.
  6. Check tire tread and wheel bearings. Uneven wear can point to alignment issues that cost more to fix than the tires themselves.
  7. Take it for a real test ride. Listen for unusual noises under acceleration, braking, and turning — not just at idle in the driveway.
  8. Ask why they're selling. A clear, consistent answer is a good sign; vague or shifting answers are worth a second look at the paperwork.

Mistakes Buyers Make in Either Direction

Comparing sticker prices without comparing financing terms

A lower price with a higher interest rate and shorter loan term can cost more overall than a new unit financed at a promotional rate. Always ask for total cost over the life of the loan.

Assuming a used unit still has warranty coverage

Never take a seller's word for remaining warranty. A VIN lookup takes minutes and tells you exactly what's covered and through what date.

Skipping the pre-purchase inspection to save time

An inspection that takes an hour can prevent a repair bill that erases the entire savings of buying used. This step is worth the time even on a private-party deal.

Overpaying for a new unit without shopping promotional rates

Manufacturer financing offers change seasonally. Buying new without checking current promotional rates can mean leaving real savings on the table.

Ignoring resale value when choosing a trim

Popular, in-demand trims hold value better than rare or heavily optioned ones. If resale matters to you, that should factor into which trim you choose — new or used.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it cheaper to buy a new or used Can-Am SxS?

The purchase price is almost always lower used, but total cost depends on financing rates and remaining warranty coverage. A used unit with a higher interest rate and no warranty left can cost more over time than a new unit financed at a promotional rate.

How much does a Can-Am SxS depreciate in the first year?

Most of the steepest depreciation happens in the first one to two years of ownership, after which the value curve flattens considerably. Popular trims with documented maintenance hold value better than base models or units with unknown service history.

Does a Can-Am warranty transfer to a used buyer?

Remaining factory warranty typically stays with the vehicle regardless of owner, but extended service contracts are not always transferable. Confirm transferability in writing before finalizing a used purchase.

Is it harder to get financing on a used SxS than a new one?

Financing is generally available for both, but used-unit loans tend to carry higher interest rates and shorter terms than new-unit promotional financing, which can offset some or all of the lower purchase price.

What should I check before buying a used Can-Am SxS?

At minimum, confirm warranty status by VIN, review full service records, inspect the belt and CVT, check the undercarriage for impact damage, and take it for a real test ride before agreeing to a price.
